\section*{Question 9}
Homoskedasticity requires
\[
\operatorname{Var}(u_i\mid\mathrm{education}_i,\mathrm{ability}_i)=\sigma^2,
\]
so the conditional error variance must be constant across individuals.
This may be implausible for wages: wage dispersion could differ across
education and ability levels because of differences in occupations,
industries, and other unobserved factors. Thus heteroskedasticity is
plausible, although this argument alone does not demonstrate that it is
present in this dataset.

Homoskedasticity is not required for OLS to be unbiased. With random
sampling, no perfect multicollinearity, and zero conditional mean,
$E[u_i\mid\mathrm{education}_i,\mathrm{ability}_i]=0$, OLS can remain
unbiased even if the conditional error variance varies. Heteroskedasticity
instead affects the validity of the usual OLS standard-error formula and
the Gauss--Markov efficiency result. In practice, heteroskedasticity-robust
standard errors can address inference, but they do not correct omitted-variable
bias. As instructed, the estimates reported in this assignment use the
usual OLS standard errors.
