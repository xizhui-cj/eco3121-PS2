\section*{Question 9}
Homoskedasticity requires
\[
\operatorname{Var}(u_i\mid\mathrm{education}_i,\mathrm{ability}_i)=\sigma^2.
\]
This may be implausible for wages: unobserved factors such as occupation
and industry may produce different wage dispersion across education and
ability levels. Heteroskedasticity is therefore plausible, though not
established by this reasoning alone.

Homoskedasticity is not required for OLS to be unbiased. With random
sampling, no perfect multicollinearity, and zero conditional mean,
$E[u_i\mid\mathrm{education}_i,\mathrm{ability}_i]=0$, OLS can remain
unbiased even if the conditional error variance varies. Homoskedasticity
is needed for the usual OLS variance formula and the Gauss--Markov
efficiency guarantee, rather than for unbiasedness.
