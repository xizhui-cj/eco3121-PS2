\section*{Question 4}
First, education is regressed on ability, including an intercept. The residuals
are saved as \texttt{res}, using the unrounded fitted values.
\input{results/q4_output}
Both regressions include an intercept and use all 200 observations.
The coefficient on \texttt{res} equals the education coefficient in Question 1
before rounding, up to numerical precision.

\paragraph{Explanation.}
The coefficient on \texttt{res} is 3.690, equal to the education coefficient
in Question 1. The residuals capture the variation in education left after
removing its linear relationship with ability. Regressing wage on these
residuals therefore gives the same education coefficient as controlling for
ability directly, as stated by the Frisch--Waugh--Lovell theorem.
