\section*{Question 8}
If ability is omitted, the regression becomes
\[
\mathrm{wage}_i=\beta_0+\beta_1\mathrm{education}_i+v_i,
\qquad v_i=\beta_2\mathrm{ability}_i+u_i.
\]
Because ability is correlated with education, omitting it can violate the
zero conditional mean assumption, $E[v_i\mid\mathrm{education}_i]=0$.
Under the full model's exogeneity assumption, the omitted-variable bias is
\[
\operatorname{plim}\tilde{\beta}_1-\beta_1
=\beta_2\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(\mathrm{education}_i,\mathrm{ability}_i)}
{\operatorname{Var}(\mathrm{education}_i)}
=\beta_2\delta_1,
\]
where $\delta_1$ is the population slope from regressing ability on education.
Since ability is expected to raise wages ($\beta_2>0$) and to be positively
associated with education ($\delta_1>0$), the bias is upward. The education
coefficient without ability is therefore expected to be larger than the
coefficient controlling for ability. The estimates, 4.760 versus 3.690,
are consistent with this prediction.
