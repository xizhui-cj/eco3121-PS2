\section*{Question 8}
If ability is omitted, the regression becomes
\[
\mathrm{wage}_i=\beta_0+\beta_1\mathrm{education}_i+v_i,
\qquad v_i=\beta_2\mathrm{ability}_i+u_i.
\]
so, Because ability is correlated with education, omitting it can violate the
zero conditional mean assumption, $E[v_i\mid\mathrm{education}_i]=0$.
the omitted-variable bias is
\[
\operatorname{plim}\tilde{\beta}_1-\beta_1
=\beta_2\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(\mathrm{education}_i,\mathrm{ability}_i)}
{\operatorname{Var}(\mathrm{education}_i)}
=\beta_2\delta_1,
\]

Since ability is expected to raise wages ($\beta_2>0$) and positively
associated with education ($\delta_1>0$), the bias is upward. The education
coefficient without ability is expected larger than the
coefficient controlling ability. The estimates, 4.760 vs. 3.690,
are consistent with this prediction.
