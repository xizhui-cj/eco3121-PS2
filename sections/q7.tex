\paragraph{(b) Interpretation.}
The estimate $\hat{\beta}_2=2.086$ means that, holding education constant,
a one-unit increase in ability is associated with a 2.086-unit increase in
hourly wage. The auxiliary slope $\tilde{\delta}_1=0.512$ means that one
additional year of education is associated with 0.512 more units of ability
in the sample.

Using unrounded estimates, their product is 1.069 wage units per year of
education. When ability is omitted, the education coefficient also captures
the wage association of ability that is correlated with education. This
confounding term accounts for the increase from $\hat{\beta}_1=3.690$ to
$\tilde{\beta}_1=4.760$. These are conditional and unconditional associations;
the numerical identity alone does not establish causation.
